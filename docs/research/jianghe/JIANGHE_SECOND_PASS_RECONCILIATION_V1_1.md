```text

-> build bottom-level logic

```

This process is now first-class evidence rather than background prose.

### 2.2 Probability/risk is a system layer, not a loose philosophy

004/019/026/064/079/089/094/097/099 jointly support:
- single outcomes do not define trade quality;
- accepted loss is a controlled cost;
- position size is constrained by tolerated loss and stop distance;
- quality/score/context can modify allocated risk;
- account-level caps/physical fund separation can bound behavioral and tail exposure;
- extreme R is a small-probability market outcome, not a normal per-trade requirement.

No universal account-risk percentage or one cross-Family mother formula is source-pinned.

### 2.3 Trend quality/stage evidence is richer

006/008/019/024/025/028/054/075/078/086/093 jointly support:
- trend is timeframe-specific;
- strength is relative and process-based, not a single candle label;
- deeper/frequent pullbacks, weaker trend candles and flatter slope can degrade trend quality;
- strong/imbalance rhythm favors breakout; weaker/deeper-pullback rhythm favors pullback;
- early vs non-early stage changes trade/exit quality, but a universal machine stage classifier remains unknown.

### 2.4 Management is more contextual, not less disciplined

005/017/019/020/021/023/025/050/073/080/090/093 show a consistent architecture:
- exit mode is defined by order mode, market rhythm and pre-trade conditions;
- dynamic structure, fixed R/R and key-location/space exits are contextual alternatives;
- personal state/schedule may justify a preplanned simpler exit, but not arbitrary P&L-driven switching;
- stalled short-term orders may be actively closed even before a hard stop.

### 2.5 Range/risk handling was underrepresented

020/021/027 plus later D-family sources show:
- outer-boundary focus;
- second-push/strength-failure confirmation;
- lighter/source-scoped sizing in several range examples;
- opposite boundary is not a mandatory target;
- a real breakout invalidates the range reversal mechanism and triggers reclassification.
