## 1. Market classification system

The source's stable top-level action taxonomy is **trend vs non-trend/range**, with important overlays for strength, stage and transition.

- Trend is timeframe-specific: any statement about a trend must specify its timeframe (019). Rising/falling high-low sequences describe directional structure; structure damage can precede full reversal confirmation (006/008/019).
- Trend quality is process-based rather than one-bar labeling. Deeper/more frequent pullbacks, smaller trend-direction candles, more counter candles and flatter slope can weaken the trend (008); strong near-straight/low-pullback conditions are different from weak/deep-pullback trends (028/075).
- Range/wide range is characterized by lack of sustained one-side dominance: both sides can move but fail to continue. External boundaries become the main action areas (021/066/069/095).
- Balance/imbalance, weak-bull/weak-bear bias inside a range, early/non-early stage and failed-break transitions are overlays rather than a single mutually exclusive taxonomy (069/078/090).
- A real range breakout causes regime reclassification rather than continuing to trade the old boundary reversal logic (015/023/027/047).

## 2. Preconditions for each market class

The second pass makes the decision hierarchy clearer:

```text
market state / timeframe
-> directional bias / rhythm
-> location and available space
-> relative strength / weakening / follow-through
-> actual execution-timeframe behavior
-> trigger
```

- Strong/imbalance rhythm with shallow pullback favors breakout or shallow trend continuation (015/028/075/086/093).
- Weak trend/deeper pullback favors waiting for pullback completion rather than chasing (028/055/075/081).
- Old-direction weakening alone is not reversal permission; new-direction takeover/follow-through is still required (012/050/068/090).
- Range mode requires confirmed external boundaries and lack of sustained continuation; middle-zone movement does not automatically become a main-TF swing setup (021/066/067/069).
- Bias is a filter, not a trigger. “Cannot long” does not automatically mean “can short,” and vice versa (050/085 and second-pass 027 support).

## 3. Key locations and how they are formed

Repeated source-defined locations include:
- swing start/end points;
- prior significant highs/lows;
- trend defense/firing/origin points;
- external range boundaries;
- breakout/retest zones;
- source-specific “trading” vs “reference” areas;
- prior-body references in some target contexts;
- “vacuum area” where obvious left-side consensus/key locations are absent.

V011 adds an important conceptual definition: a key location is a boundary between two market moves / a prior battle-result area. V007 and V006 place location below market/trend context and above isolated form. **Location creates observation/permission context, never automatic entry.** Exact zone width and point-picking algorithm remain UNKNOWN.

## 4. Price/K-line behavior observed at locations

The source repeatedly reads behavior comparatively and sequentially:
- body vs wick share and whole-candle size;
- relative strength against adjacent candles;
- momentum strengthening/weakening;
- engulf/rejection;
- follow-through after a break;
- inability to make a new extreme;
- second/multiple-push failure;
- slope/tempo change;
- horizontal overlap/contest;
- strong-weak-strong transitions and “contest state.”

V006 makes explicit that historical candles provide trend/location context while current candles provide current momentum. V078 later formalizes body/wick proportions but creates an exact-50 overlap; this remains UNKNOWN. V007/078 explicitly support **contest/overlap = wait**, not “see a pattern and act.”

## 5. Entry timing and trigger

Core entry semantics still group into four mechanisms:

### A — trend pullback continuation
Qualified trend + key/defense location + relatively weak counter-pullback + trend-direction recovery/confirmation. V024 provides explicit ABC source-local rules; V025 shows breakout-vs-pullback entry selected by follow-through after the break.

### B — weakening reversal / HTF pullback
Old direction weakens/fails + high-value context + new direction actually takes control. First counter candle or mere weakening is insufficient.

### C — confirmed breakout
Price genuinely leaves old space + follow-through / lower-TF second confirmation where used. V015/023/025/027 now provide much richer source evidence. Second confirmation improves quality but is not a guarantee.

### D — range-boundary reversal
Confirmed range + external boundary + second/multiple-push failure or strength conversion + counter confirmation. Boundary arrival alone is insufficient.

Across all: `PATTERN_NAME != TRADE_PERMISSION`; N, engulf, wick or firing-point label alone is never a complete reason.

## 6. Explicit no-trade conditions

First-class no-trade evidence now includes:
- location reached but no actual reaction/trigger (005/011/018/095/098);
- unresolved overlap/contest (007/078);
- range middle for main-TF swing logic (021/067/069);
- chase entry with poor stop/RR or near opposite boundary (055/068/081);
- old direction weakened but new direction lacks burst/follow-through (068);
- intact strong trend countertrend attempt just to catch the unique top/bottom (085);
- failed breakout/no follow/rapid return to old space (015/023/027);
- rule conditions not met even if market later moves (050/070);
- emotional/revenge/P&L-driven action outside system conditions (013/094/096);
- no-stop/martingale/lucky-all-in paths as non-reproducible hard negatives (004/083).

## 7. How the trader admits being wrong

Invalidation is primarily **structural/logical**, not account-P&L based:
- relevant swing/defense point fails;
- trend cannot continue and a prior defense is broken;
- breakout rapidly returns to old space / fails continuation;
- range boundary genuinely breaks with follow-through;
- new-direction reversal thesis fails and old direction resumes.

V017 explicitly frames dynamic exit around pre-defining what market behavior would negate the holding thesis. V079's “entry-candle close stop” branch remains unusable because source text/visual examples conflict. A stop-out does not permanently ban another attempt; a **new independent setup** can restore permission (025/027/061).

## 8. Management after the trade works

Three source-supported exit families are explicit (017):
1. fixed R/R;
2. key-location / available-space exit;
3. dynamic market-structure exit.

The trader personally favors dynamic structure in V017, but the corpus shows legitimate contextual alternatives:
- no preset TP and update structure as price moves (005);
- proactive range exit before opposite boundary when momentum/slope changes (021/066);
- fixed target due order mode or personal schedule/state (020/058/090);
- prior-high/body reference target in weak/range context (057/059);
- micro-swing/scalp exits judged by predeclared order mode (073/080);
- active exit when a short-term order stalls even before hard stop (093).

Trend stage matters: early trend can be given more room; non-early trend can protect earlier (017). This resolves the semantic conflict but not the machine stage classifier.

## 9. Position sizing and risk process

This area becomes substantially richer after the second pass.

Stable source hierarchy:
- accepted loss/cost is more controllable than profit (004/019/097);
- stop distance and tolerated loss constrain position size; V079 gives the explicit formula `position = tolerated loss / (stop points × point value)`;
- quality/score/context can modify allocated risk (002/026/030/055/061/099);
- imperfect/left-side/no-confluence/range attempts are often lighter in source cases;
- planned staged entries/additions require new conditions and should not be confused with uncontrolled averaging down (020/021/024/025/081);
- account-layer controls can cap daily tradable funds or physically separate excess capital (094/099).

Important boundaries:
- V021's 0.5x range sizing is a source-era/local rule, not a permanent D-family constant.
- V019's >=1:1 habit is a source-era personal baseline, not a universal R/R gate.
- V030 exact score=3 permission remains internally inconsistent.
- no universal account-risk percentage or one unified cross-Family mother formula is source-pinned.

## 10. Multi-timeframe coordination

The source repeatedly separates roles:
- higher/main TF: market state, direction, key area, remaining space;
- lower/execution TF: actual strength change, structure and entry timing.

Examples include 4H→1H→15m→5m/1m and 1H→5m→1m. Crucially:
- every timeframe has its own trend/structure (019);
- same-TF internal structure may help confirm/weakening/reclassification/management but is **not** standalone entry permission (018/069);
- if a lower-TF setup is required, actually switch to that TF and identify its own structure; do not relabel same-TF internal structure as the lower TF's external structure.
- confluence is a quality modifier, not a universal hard prerequisite (058/060).

- old direction weakening without new-direction takeover;
